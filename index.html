Research

Unions in the Age of Automation
with A. Arda Gitmez and Özlem Kına
Working Paper

Automation pulls on workers in two directions. On one hand, it can depress wages and weaken unions; on the other, the productivity gains it delivers expand labor-market opportunities that indirectly benefit workers. We build a model to determine which channel dominates and to characterize the policies a regulator might consider.

We show that at intermediate productivity levels, firms use the threat of automation to deter workers from unionizing, suppressing wages while automating little on the equilibrium path. At sufficiently high productivity, this deterrence breaks down, and unionization, wages, and labor-market opportunities recover.

Focusing on the intermediate region, we compare two policies. Capping automation intensity eliminates the deterrence effect and restores unionization and wages, but it reduces vacancy creation and can raise realized automation above the deterred baseline. Union-provided employment protection, combined with higher wages, reproduces the on-path allocation of a full automation ban while leaving non-union matches free to automate.
